why mine at ghost

A better deal for hashers.

Ghost Pool was built on the idea that miners should keep more of what they earn, get paid directly on-chain, and never hand their credentials to an operator.

  • 1% fee, subsidy only
    The pool takes 1% of the block subsidy — split 50/50 between treasury and the node reward pool. Zero cut of transaction fees.
  • 100% of TX fees to the winner
    When a block is found, transaction fees are paid in full to the node that built the winning template — no haircut to the pool, no hidden skim.
  • Ledger-style payouts
    Every share you submit accumulates on your unpaid ledger until you're paid. Shares never evaporate from template rolls — your work carries forward across blocks.
  • On-chain, non-custodial
    Payouts land in the coinbase of every block Ghost finds — direct to your Bitcoin address. The pool never takes custody of your sats.
  • Stratum V1 and V2
    Run what your ASIC already speaks. SV1 on port 3333 for legacy hardware, SV2 on 34255 for encrypted transport and per-miner channels. Miners hash against the node's template — Ghost doesn't run job declaration, so transaction selection stays with the node operator.
  • Any SHA-256 ASIC
    Bitaxe, Whatsminer, Antminer, Avalon — if it hashes double-SHA256 and talks Stratum, it works. No vendor lock-in, no firmware restrictions.
  • No registration, no KYC
    There's no account to create. Your payout address IS your identity. Plug in, authorise with <your-address>.<worker>, and start hashing.
  • Solo or pooled, your call
    Run your own node and mine against the templates your node builds — or point at someone else's public-mining node and pool up. Same protocol, same payouts either way.

quickstart

Connect a miner in seconds.

No account. No sign-up. No KYC. Your payout address is your identity — paste it in, pick your Stratum version, and copy the exact settings for your miner.

Stratum version

One address can back as many workers as you like — give each ASIC its own worker name. Shares land on your unpaid ledger within seconds. Track them any time by pasting your address into the lookup on the pool page — no login.

The settings above point at the public pool — open to anyone, no permission needed. Some operators instead run a private pool you join with a password they give you, or mine solo on their own node. Those are choices the node operator makes — see the three mining modes if you want to run your own node and decide how its hashpower is used.

the mesh

Every public node, live.

pool.bitcoinghost.org is a convenience, not the pool — it balances you across nodes. The pool is the mesh below, and you can point a miner at any node in it directly. Nothing here is a hardcoded list: the page asks a node, and the node reports itself and every peer it is connected to, so a node that joins tonight appears here on its own.

Asking the mesh…

SV1 works against any node on the list, and against pool.bitcoinghost.org — it authenticates nothing, so the address you point at does not have to be a specific machine. SV2 is different: it pins the node's authority key, every node has its own, and so an SV2 miner has to name one node. The quickstart above will give you a node's host and its matching key together.

Picking a node yourself is the point, not a workaround — it is what keeps the pool from depending on one name resolving. Your payouts are identical wherever you connect: shares are pooled across the whole mesh and paid against your address, not against the node that happened to take them.
