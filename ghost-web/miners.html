firmware compatibility

Most hardware can't carry 500 payouts.

A block pays miners from its coinbase, and a 500-output coinbase is about 22 KB. Most firmware cannot safely take that, and on Bitmain and Avalon firmware an oversized coinbase can corrupt memory or be silently skipped rather than refused. So each job carries a slot class sized to the miner's firmware. Your credit doesn't depend on it; how many balances a block you find can pay does.

From published source, official firmware images and OCEAN's public blocks. None of it was measured on our own hardware; ceilings are raised only by tests. Outputs assume P2TR payees. A miner only gets a class whose whole coinbase fits under its ceiling: the classes are 3, 9, 44, 143, 364 and 500 outputs (500 B, 730 B, 2.25 KB, 6.5 KB, 16 KB and 22 KB), so an S21 fits the 44 class and a stock S19 the 9 class.

Stratum V2 avoids the limit. On a standard SV2 channel the miner receives only a header, so it always gets the full 500-slot class. Only SV2 firmware such as Braiins OS can use it; stock Antminers speak SV1 only.

Known low-limit firmware is warned, not refused. The node tells you at connect and on the miner page, naming the model and what the limit is expected to cost. Blocks it finds pay fewer balances and send more to the treasury as change.

the sensing loop

The node feels its way up.

A valid share proves your firmware built that coinbase. Nothing proves what it can't carry. So each connection is sensed:

  1. 1
    Identify
    The node looks your model up by its user agent (SV1) or the vendor and firmware fields of the connection (SV2). An unknown miner gets the smallest class only.
  2. 2
    Start small
    Every miner starts at the smallest class, 3 outputs.
  3. 3
    Step up
    After 20 valid shares at a class, the node tries the next class up.
  4. 4
    Never past the ceiling
    It never tries a class above your model's known-safe ceiling in the table, however well the miner is doing.
  5. 5
    Drop back on failure
    If the miner disconnects or stops returning valid shares while a larger class is being tried, the node drops back to the last class that worked and stays there. Once settled, a dropped connection resumes at the settled class.
getting paid

Credited now, paid when a block lands.

Each share adds to your balance at once. Each block found pays balances of at least 1,000 sats from the table as it stood 20 blocks earlier: half its slots to the balances waiting longest, half to the largest, exactly what is owed and never more. The payout rule →

Check your balance on the miner page. Checking that your node pays you honestly needs ghost-audit, which is built but not released yet.
