credited shares

What you earned.

Only shares at or above the network tier are sent between nodes and credited. A share is credited once an anchor's batch includes it, about a minute after it is found.
how you get paid

In plain words.

Your miner's username is your payout address. Every share it finds that reaches the network tier is credited to that address, in sats worked out from the share's own difficulty. Every node runs the same arithmetic on the same share chain, so every node shows the same balance.

When the pool finds a block, the coinbase pays miners directly. Of the subsidy, 0.5% goes to the treasury and 99.5% pays balances, exactly as they stood 20 blocks earlier. All transaction fees go to the node whose miner found the block. Nobody holds your money in between.

Half of a block's miner outputs go to the oldest balances (those that reached 1,000 sats first), half to the largest. The largest balance of all is paid last, with whatever is left. Nobody is paid more than they are owed, and a balance falls only by what a block actually paid it. Balances under 1,000 sats wait. When a block cannot fill its outputs, the change goes to the treasury.

Firmware matters. A block can only pay as many balances as the coinbase the finding miner's firmware can carry: a stock Antminer S19 about 9, an S21 about 44, Whatsminer and Avalon about 135-143, Braiins OS and ePIC 364 or more, and SV2 firmware on a standard channel the full 500. Each node senses what a miner can carry and never sends it more. A low-limit miner is warned about, not refused: the blocks it finds pay fewer balances and send the rest to the treasury, so everyone waits longer. Your own balance is never reduced by it.

Waits can be long. Without an audit with ghost-audit, you are trusting the node you mine on to credit you.
