Your miner.

Enter the payout address your miner uses as its username. You get your balance, every worker's hashrate and shares, your place in the payout queue with an honest wait, and every payout with its block.

A link to ?address=<address> can be bookmarked or shared; add &worker=<name> to open on one worker.

what this means

In plain words.

Your balance. Your miner's username is your payout address. Every share it finds at or above the network tier is credited to that address, in sats worked out from the share's own difficulty. Every node runs the same arithmetic on the same share chain, so every node shows the same balance.

Hashrate and shares. Your miner sends shares at its own difficulty; the page counts them and works out a hashrate from their difficulty. Stale shares were mined on a block that had just been replaced; a few are normal. Rejected shares failed a check, and the reason is listed. Only the larger shares that reach the network tier earn credit, so a worker can show hashrate before its balance moves.

Getting paid. When the pool finds a block, the coinbase pays miners directly. Of the subsidy, 0.5% goes to the treasury and 99.5% pays balances as they stood 20 blocks earlier. Half of a block's miner outputs go to the oldest balances (those that reached 1,000 sats first), half to the largest; the largest of all is paid last with whatever is left. Nobody is paid more than they are owed, and nobody holds your money in between. Transaction fees go to the node whose miner found the block.

The wait is an estimate. Blocks are found at random. The page uses how often the pool found blocks recently and how many balances they paid; when there is too little to go on, it says unknown instead of inventing a number. Waits can be long.

Firmware matters. A block can only pay as many balances as the coinbase the finding miner's firmware can carry: a stock Antminer S19 about 9, an S21 about 44, Whatsminer and Avalon about 135-143, Braiins OS and ePIC 364 or more, SV2 on a standard channel the full 500. Each node senses what a miner can carry (its slot class) and never sends it more. A low-limit miner is warned about, not refused; your own balance is never reduced by it.

Trust. Without an audit with ghost-audit, you are trusting the node you mine on to credit you.
